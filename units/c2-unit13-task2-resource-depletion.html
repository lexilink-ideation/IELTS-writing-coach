C2 Task 2

Unit 13: Natural Resource Depletion — Full Essay Practice

Skill focus: Full Problem-Solution Essay

1 Mini Lesson

This unit brings together everything the curriculum has built toward: a complete, four-paragraph Problem-Solution essay written at full length. The question gives you two clear jobs — explain the problems a trend causes, then propose solutions — and a strong response keeps these two jobs cleanly separated across two body paragraphs.

Paragraph 1 (Introduction): paraphrase the question + state you will cover problems, then solutions. Paragraph 2 (Problems): one well-developed real-world example per point. Paragraph 3 (Solutions): each solution answers a problem raised above. Paragraph 4 (Conclusion): summarize + a brief, confident final opinion.

Three habits separate a Band 8-9 response from a Band 6-7 one at this level:

  • Use one well-developed example per point rather than listing several superficially — depth beats breadth for the "extend and support ideas" criterion.
  • Make sure each solution directly answers a problem raised earlier — examiners actively check for this coherence.
  • Keep real-world examples specific: name the place, a date or timeframe, and a concrete consequence — numbers help.
  • Avoid memorized "template" phrases the examiner has likely seen hundreds of times — paraphrase naturally instead.

Aim for four paragraphs and 260-300 words total.

2 Model Essay

This is a complete worked answer to the exact question below. Read it closely, then write your own response to the same question in the Writing Box — using different real-world examples than the ones used here.

Question: Natural resources such as oil, forests and fresh water are being consumed at an alarming rate. What problems does this cause? How can these problems be solved?

Resources such as petroleum, timber, and drinking water are being consumed far more quickly than they can be replenished. This essay will examine the negative consequences of this trend before proposing several measures that could help to address it.

Overreliance on oil creates serious economic and environmental risks, since extraction and transport accidents can cause lasting damage. The 2010 Deepwater Horizon spill in the Gulf of Mexico, for instance, released an estimated four million barrels of crude oil over 87 days, devastating marine wildlife and crippling the region's fishing and tourism industries for years afterward. Deforestation poses an equally severe threat: the Amazon rainforest has already lost 17-20% of its original cover, and the extensive fires linked to cattle ranching in 2019 raised fears that the forest is approaching an irreversible tipping point at which it could transform into savanna, destroying biodiversity and a crucial carbon sink in the process. Freshwater scarcity is perhaps the most immediately dangerous consequence of all: Cape Town came within weeks of "Day Zero" in 2018, the point at which its municipal taps would have been shut off entirely, while the Aral Sea, once the world's fourth-largest lake, has shrunk by more than 90% since the 1960s after Soviet irrigation projects diverted its feeder rivers, collapsing the local fishing economy.

Fortunately, several measures could mitigate these problems, each answering one of the difficulties raised above. Government policy can directly incentivize conservation, as shown by Costa Rica's payment-for-ecosystem-services scheme, which pays landowners to reforest degraded land; as a result, forest cover has risen from around 21% in the 1980s to over 50% today. Technological innovation offers similar promise: Israel now recycles an estimated 85-90% of its wastewater for agricultural irrigation, dramatically easing pressure on its limited freshwater supply, while investment in renewable energy reduces dependence on oil in much the same way. Finally, individual behavioral change, such as reduced consumption and sustainable purchasing, can shift long-term demand, although this is most effective when paired with the policy and technological measures above rather than relied upon alone.

In conclusion, the depletion of oil, forests, and fresh water is already causing severe economic, environmental, and social harm, from collapsed fishing economies to disappearing rainforest. Of the solutions available, government policy paired with technological innovation appears the most promising, since, unlike individual behavior change alone, it can produce rapid, large-scale results.

4 Self-Review Checklist

  • My introduction paraphrases the question and previews problems, then solutions, without giving opinions or examples yet.
  • My problems paragraph covers oil, forests, and fresh water, each with one well-developed, specific example (place, timeframe, concrete consequence).
  • My solutions paragraph directly answers each problem I raised, rather than gesturing vaguely at "going green".
  • My conclusion summarizes the main problem and most promising solution, plus a brief, confident opinion, without introducing new examples.
  • I used different real-world examples than the model essay above.
  • I avoided memorized template phrases and paraphrased naturally instead.
  • I wrote at least 250 words across four paragraphs.
