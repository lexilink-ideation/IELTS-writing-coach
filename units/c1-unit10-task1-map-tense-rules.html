Unit 10: Map Report: Choosing the Right Tense

Skill focus: Task 1 — Map Tense Rules

1 Mini Lesson

Map reports have their own tense rules. Use past tense for a map showing the past or present state, and future forms ("will be") for a map showing a planned future. When one report compares both, mix the tenses — and reach for the passive voice, since planners, not the map itself, are the implied agent of each change.

The ___ (feature) was ___ (past description), but it will be ___ (future change) by ___ (year).

Example: "the office was located... but will be moved..." — past for what existed, future passive for what is planned.

2 Model Sentences

This example uses a different topic (a coastal town's flood defences) so you can see the pattern — then apply it to the new map below.

  • The seafront was an open, unprotected beach, but it will be reinforced with a concrete flood barrier by 2035, according to the city's new development plan.

3 Guided Practice

Fill in the gaps using the changes in the table below (not the model sentence above):

The village centre was ___ (past description), but it will be ___ (future change). To the north, the open farmland was ___, yet it will be ___. The natural riverbank will be ___ into a public park.

5 Self-Review Checklist

  • I used past tense ("was" / "were") for the current features.
  • I used the future passive ("will be") for the planned changes.
  • I used a precise change verb in the passive, e.g. "expanded", "built", or "converted".
  • I mentioned the village, the housing estate, and the public park.
  • I used a contrast connector such as "but", "however", or "yet" to link the two tenses.
