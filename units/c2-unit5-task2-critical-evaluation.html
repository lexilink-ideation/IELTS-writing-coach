Unit 5: Critical Evaluation Essay

Skill focus: Task 2 — Critical Evaluation

1 Mini Lesson

A genuine critical evaluation weighs real strengths and weaknesses on both sides of an issue before reaching a conclusion — it never simply endorses one option as flatly superior.

___ (option 1) offers ___ (strength), yet it also entails ___ (weakness). By contrast, ___ (option 2) provides ___ (strength), although it may ___ (weakness). On balance, ___ (nuanced conclusion weighing both).

"On balance" signals that your conclusion has genuinely weighed both sides rather than picking a side from the outset.

2 Model Sentences

This example uses a different topic (online classes vs. traditional classes) so you can see the pattern — then apply it to the new topic below.

  • Online classes offer considerable flexibility, allowing students to learn at their own pace and fit study around other commitments, yet this same flexibility can also foster procrastination and a lack of structured accountability. By contrast, traditional classroom learning provides consistent structure and immediate peer interaction, although it may disadvantage students who cannot easily attend a fixed schedule. On balance, the most effective approach may depend less on the format itself and more on a given student's capacity for self-directed learning.

3 Guided Practice

Fill in the gaps using the topic below (not the model paragraph above):

Studying abroad offers ___ (strength), yet it also entails ___ (weakness). By contrast, studying locally provides ___ (strength), although it may ___ (weakness). On balance, ___ (nuanced conclusion).

5 Self-Review Checklist

  • I introduced studying locally with a contrast connector such as "by contrast".
  • I closed with a balanced conclusion phrase such as "on balance".
