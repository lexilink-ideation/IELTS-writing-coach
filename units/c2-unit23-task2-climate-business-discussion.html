C2 Task 2

Unit 23: Does Climate Change Damage Business or Create Opportunity? — A Philosophical Approach

Skill focus: Full Discussion Essay (Discuss Both Views + Opinion, Philosophical Reasoning)

1 Mini Lesson

This is a Discussion (Discuss Both Views + Opinion) essay on one of the hardest prompt families in the exam. The task type matters: "Discuss both views" is not the same as "agree or disagree." Both views must be developed fairly, each in its own paragraph and presented at its strongest, before your own opinion is defended. An essay that argues one side and waves the other away is capped at Band 6 for Task Response, however good the English. A Band 9 response weighs two competing accounts of what a changing climate does to commerce — the destruction of value versus the creation of new markets — before landing on a clear, qualified position.

A second warning specific to this question: it is a business question, not an environment question. Melting ice caps and endangered species earn nothing unless you convert them into a commercial consequence — a cost, a price, a supply chain, a market. This is the most common way strong candidates lose marks on climate prompts.

Paragraph 1 (Introduction): background statement (paraphrase) + signal both views + state your position. Paragraph 2 (View A): climate change destroys value and, worse, makes risk impossible to price, one specific example. Paragraph 3 (View B): disruption is how markets create — a constraint is also an instruction, one specific example. Paragraph 4 (Your position): separate value created from value merely transferred, and ask who gains and who loses, one specific example. Paragraph 5 (Conclusion): restate the tension + confident, qualified final opinion, no new examples.

Four habits separate a Band 8-9 response from a Band 6-7 one at this level:

  • Develop both views fairly. Give the view you ultimately reject a full, sympathetic paragraph — present it at its strongest, never as a straw man.
  • State your position in the introduction as well as the conclusion. Examiners look for a clear, consistent stance throughout, not a verdict saved for the last sentence.
  • Name a principle in plain English (unpriceable risk, created versus transferred, activity is not value) — this makes the reasoning visible and supports both coherence and task response.
  • Use one well-developed real-world example per body paragraph rather than several vague ones — specificity (names, places, figures) beats breadth.

Aim for four to five paragraphs and 270-310 words total.

2 Essay Outline

Essay Structure at a Glance

  • Introduction — paraphrase the question, signal both views, and state your position.
  • Body Paragraph 1 (View A) — climate change destroys value, and worse, makes risk impossible to price: assets are damaged, but the deeper harm is that firms lose the ability to calculate.
  • Body Paragraph 2 (View B) — disruption is how markets create: a constraint is also an instruction, and the response to climate change is now the largest investment story in the world.
  • Body Paragraph 3 (Your position) — separate value created from value merely transferred, and ask who gains and who loses.
  • Conclusion — restate the tension + a confident, qualified final opinion.

1. Introduction

Paraphrase the prompt, then signal that you will weigh two competing accounts of what a changing climate does to commerce — the destruction of value versus the creation of new markets — before landing on a clear position. Your opinion should be visible here, not saved as a surprise for the conclusion.

Example paraphrase: "Insurers are quietly withdrawing from coastlines at the same moment as shipyards fill with orders for wind-turbine installation vessels. Some observers therefore read a warming climate as a straightforward threat to commerce, while others see in it the largest set of commercial opportunities since the arrival of the internet. This essay will examine both accounts before arguing that climate change generates an enormous amount of business activity while creating remarkably little new wealth, because most of what it produces is either defensive spending or a transfer from those who suffer to those who supply the remedy."

2. Body Paragraph 1 — View A: The Bill Was Always Coming, and It Cannot Be Priced

The first view rests on the observation that every business depends on physical conditions it does not control and has never had to pay for. Two distinct harms follow. The obvious one is destruction: crops fail, factories flood, assets are written off. The subtler and more serious one is the loss of calculability. Commerce can absorb a known hazard, because a known hazard can be insured, priced into a contract and carried as a line in the accounts. It cannot absorb a hazard whose distribution is shifting, because there is no longer a stable past from which to estimate the future. When risk becomes uncertainty, capital does not demand a higher premium; it leaves.

Philosophical concept: Frank Knight's distinction between risk, which is measurable and therefore insurable, and uncertainty, which is not — a distinction that explains why firms withdraw from a market rather than simply charging more. Behind it sits Pigou's idea of the externality: the damage was never new, merely unbilled, so what looks like a sudden shock is in fact a deferred cost arriving. Hardin's tragedy of the commons completes the account by explaining why no individual firm ever had an incentive to prevent a cost that everybody shared.

Example: On 26 May 2023 State Farm, the largest home insurer in California, stopped accepting new applications for home and certain commercial property policies in the state, citing climate-driven catastrophe exposure, rising reinsurance costs and construction inflation. In January 2025 the Los Angeles wildfires produced roughly US$53 billion of losses, of which about US$40 billion was insured — according to Munich Re, the costliest wildfire disaster ever recorded. The order of events is the argument: the actuaries left before the fire, not after it. Nothing about the houses had changed; what had changed was anyone's ability to calculate the odds. And because insurance is the mechanism by which every other business converts uncertainty into a manageable cost, mortgages, construction and investment retreat behind it.

Developing the point — from the balance sheet to the supply chain. The strongest version of this view notices that climate risk does not stay with the firms physically in harm's way. It travels, because modern supply chains run through a small number of narrow places. A drought in one country therefore prices goods in another, and a company with no assets, customers or staff anywhere near the disruption still finds its costs set by it. Extending the paragraph in this direction shows development rather than repetition, and it moves the argument from the sympathetic case of the flooded farmer to the harder case of the untouched manufacturer.

Example: Drought in the Panama Canal watershed forced the authority to cut daily transits from about 36 to 22 in late 2023. Over the 2024 financial year the canal handled 9,936 transits against 12,638 the year before, a fall of 29 per cent, with dry bulk down 46 per cent and LNG carriers down 66 per cent. Rainfall in central Panama became a line item in freight budgets on three continents.

The limits of the argument matter, and stating them earns credit for judgement. No individual drought, storm or fire can be attributed to climate change with confidence, and weather has disrupted trade since trade began; the defensible claim concerns frequency and predictability, not any single event. It is also worth conceding that business has always adapted to physical risk and is often good at it. Make the modest claim — that the hazards are becoming harder to price — and leave the apocalyptic one alone.

3. Body Paragraph 2 — View B: Disruption Is How Markets Create

The opposing view begins from the observation that a constraint is also an instruction. When a resource becomes scarce or a practice becomes expensive, the price system converts a physical problem into a commercial one and pays whoever solves it. On this account climate change does not so much destroy value as relocate it: it kills the industries built on cheap emissions and enriches the ones that replace them, and what the first view records as loss is simply the first half of a process whose second half is invention.

Philosophical concept: Schumpeter's creative destruction, which holds that destruction and creation are not two events but one seen from opposite ends — the single most useful idea in this essay, because it explains why both views can describe the same world and disagree. Hayek adds why the process needs no central direction: prices transmit dispersed knowledge, so a scarcity or a carbon charge tells millions of firms that something must change without anyone specifying what. Hicks's theory of induced innovation completes it, arguing that a shift in relative costs steers the direction of invention itself.

Example: Ørsted began life as Danish Oil and Natural Gas, one of the most fossil-intensive utilities in Europe. It sold its upstream oil and gas business in 2017, renamed itself in the same year, closed its last coal-fired plant in 2024, and now generates around 99 per cent of its power from renewable sources, having built more than 18 GW of renewable capacity in the process. The scale of the wider shift is now hard to dismiss: the International Energy Agency put global energy investment at about US$3.3 trillion in 2025, of which roughly US$2.2 trillion went to clean energy — twice the sum going to fossil fuels — with solar photovoltaics alone attracting some US$450 billion, the single largest line in world energy investment.

Developing the point — and naming what the opportunity actually rests on. The same company supplies the limit of the argument, which is why it is worth choosing. In August 2025 Ørsted announced a rights issue of DKK 60 billion, about US$9.4 billion, underwritten by the Danish state as its majority shareholder, and the shares fell more than a quarter in a day. The cause was not weather but politics: American opposition to offshore wind made it impossible to sell down a stake in its Sunrise Wind project as planned. The lesson is precise and worth stating in one sentence — the opportunity is created by the political decision to respond to climate change, not by climate change itself, and political decisions reverse. A student who says this has not weakened the paragraph; they have shown they understand what the market is made of.

4. Body Paragraph 3 — Your Position: Value Created versus Value Transferred

The dispute dissolves once we ask what is actually being counted. Three quite different things are routinely filed under "business opportunity." The first is genuine innovation, which leaves the world holding capabilities it did not have before. The second is defensive expenditure, which restores what was destroyed or prevents its destruction. The third is transfer, in which one party's loss becomes another party's gain and nothing new is produced at all. Only the first makes anybody better off. A sea wall, an insurance premium and an air conditioner all generate turnover, employ people and enlarge the national accounts, yet none of them leaves us richer than we would have been had the problem never arisen. The reasonable position, then, is not to deny that climate change creates opportunities — it obviously does — but to insist on asking which of the three kinds any particular opportunity is.

Philosophical concept: Bastiat's broken window, and the distinction between the seen and the unseen: the repair is visible and the alternative use of the money is not, which is why damage is so easily mistaken for stimulus. The fallacy of composition explains the second confusion — what is true of one firm need not be true of the system, so every solar manufacturer on earth can prosper while total output falls. Rawls's difference principle supplies the moral half: an arrangement is judged by its effect on the worst-off, and here the losses and the gains land on different people, with the losses concentrated among those least able to carry them.

Example: Poor West African harvests drove world cocoa prices to a record of roughly US$10.97 per kilogram in April 2024. The farmers whose trees had failed received almost none of it: the regulated farmgate price in Côte d'Ivoire stood at FCFA 1,000 per kilogram, about US$1.71, and in Ghana at GH¢20.93, about US$4.70, until governments raised them by around 50 and 58 per cent respectively that same month — still a fraction of the world price. Confectioners absorbed higher costs, traders holding stock made fortunes, and not one additional bean existed anywhere on earth as a result. Enormous commercial opportunity was generated by a failure of supply, and the people who bore the failure were not the people who captured the opportunity.

5. Conclusion

Restate the tension between destruction and invention, then give a brief, confident, qualified opinion — for example, that climate change is undeniably generating vast commercial activity, but that activity should not be mistaken for prosperity, since most of it repairs damage or moves money from the harmed to the prepared. The useful question for a company or a government is therefore not whether opportunities exist, but whether a particular opportunity leaves the world holding something it did not have before.

6. Handling the Counter-Argument (an optional Band 8 move)

The strongest objection to the position above is that defensive spending can harden into genuine capability. A country forced to protect itself may end up with an industry it can sell to everyone else, in which case the expenditure was not merely a repair bill but an investment. The Netherlands is the standing example: centuries of compulsory flood engineering became an export trade, with Dutch water-sector exports reaching roughly €12.2 billion in 2025, about 2.3 per cent of all Dutch exports, and Dutch dredging and delta-engineering firms now advising on coastal defence from New Orleans to Jakarta. The reply is to concede the case and then confine it — capability of this kind is real but rare, arises only where adaptation is run as a long-term industrial project rather than emergency repair, and still does not show that the country is richer than it would have been had it never needed the dykes. Raising an objection and answering it in two or three sentences is one of the clearest signals of a Band 8 Task Response, but only attempt it if you can do it briefly.

Key Philosophical Concepts — Quick Reference

  • Risk versus uncertainty — a known hazard can be priced and insured; a shifting one cannot, and capital responds to uncertainty by leaving rather than by charging more (Knight).
  • Deferred costs, not new ones — the damage was always real, simply unbilled, so what looks like a shock is an old externality arriving (Pigou).
  • Tragedy of the commons — why no single firm ever had an incentive to prevent a cost that everybody shared (Hardin).
  • Creative destruction — destruction and creation are one process seen from opposite ends, which is why both views can describe the same world (Schumpeter).
  • Prices as information — scarcity turns a physical fact into a signal that redirects millions of decisions without anyone issuing an instruction (Hayek), and shifts in relative cost steer the direction of invention itself (Hicks).
  • The seen and the unseen — money spent repairing damage is not wealth created but wealth diverted from whatever it would otherwise have done (Bastiat's broken window).
  • Fallacy of composition — every green firm can prosper while total output falls; what is true of the part is not true of the whole.
  • Creation, defence and transfer — three things routinely counted as opportunity, only the first of which leaves anyone better off.
  • The difference principle — judge an arrangement by its effect on the worst-off, and notice that the losers and the winners here are not the same people (Rawls).
  • Activity is not value — turnover, employment and GDP all rise after a disaster, which is a defect in the measurement rather than a benefit of the disaster.
  • Opportunity is policy-made — the market for climate solutions exists because governments decided it should, which is precisely why it can be closed again.

Reminder: do not name philosophers or economists in the exam. The marks are for the reasoning, not the citation — "rebuilding a factory restores what was lost; it does not add anything" scores, while "as Bastiat argued" does not, and unattributed ideas read as your own thinking.

3 Band 9 Sample Essay

This is a complete Band 9 worked answer to the exact question below, written in the background-statement-plus-thesis / topic-sentence / restated-opinion format, in roughly 300 words. Read it closely, then write your own response to the same question in the Writing Box — using different real-world examples than the ones used here.

Question: Some people believe that climate change has negative effects on business, while others think it creates new business opportunities. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Insurers are retreating from exposed coastlines as shipyards fill with orders for wind-turbine vessels. To some, a warming climate threatens commerce; to others it is the largest commercial opening since the internet. Both are real, though climate change generates vast activity but little new wealth.

Every firm depends on physical conditions none has paid for. The visible harm is destruction; the deeper one is the loss of calculability. An estimable hazard is insurable, and insurable cost is just a line in the accounts; a shifting one cannot be priced, so capital withdraws rather than repricing. State Farm stopped issuing new Californian home policies in 2023, two years before wildfires there caused US$40 billion in insured losses. The actuaries left before the fire, not after it.

The opposing case holds that a constraint is also an instruction. Once a practice becomes expensive, the price system turns a physical problem into a commercial one and pays whoever solves it; industries built on cheap emissions contract as replacements expand. Ørsted sold its oil and gas division in 2017 and now runs almost entirely on renewables; clean energy drew US$2.2 trillion globally in 2025, twice fossil fuels' share. Loss, on this reading, is the first half of a process whose second half is invention.

The disagreement dissolves once we ask what is counted. Innovation leaves the world holding capabilities it lacked; defensive spending restores what was destroyed; transfer merely moves one party's loss to another. Only the first makes anybody better off, though all three swell the national accounts. When failed harvests drove cocoa to a record US$10.97 per kilogram in 2024, traders prospered, Ivorian farmers received a fraction, and not one additional bean resulted.

In conclusion, climate change generates enormous activity, but activity is not prosperity. The question worth asking of any opportunity is whether it leaves the world holding something it lacked before.

4 Key Vocabulary from the Sample Essay

English-to-English definitions of useful words and phrases from the Band 9 essay above — target vocabulary you can reuse in your own response.

  • to retreat from exposed coastlines — to stop doing business in high-risk places altogether rather than adjusting prices; a precise, commercial way to open a climate essay.
  • to threaten commerce — to endanger business activity; the compact verb phrase for View A.
  • a commercial opening — a newly available chance to make money; more precise than "opportunity" and instantly signals View B.
  • calculability — the property of being possible to measure and estimate; the abstract noun at the centre of this essay.
  • an estimable hazard — a danger whose likelihood can be worked out from past experience, and therefore insured.
  • insurable — able to be covered by insurance because the risk can be measured.
  • just a line in the accounts — a cost so predictable it becomes ordinary bookkeeping; the image that shows why priceable risk is survivable.
  • capital withdraws rather than repricing — investors leave the market instead of charging more; the exact commercial consequence of unmeasurable risk, and the heart of View A.
  • insured losses — the portion of total damage an insurance company must actually pay out.
  • a constraint is also an instruction — a memorable formulation of View B: a limit tells the market what to invent next.
  • the price system — the mechanism by which prices signal scarcity and redirect what firms do; the engine behind View B.
  • industries built on cheap emissions — businesses that were profitable only because polluting was free.
  • to contract / to expand — to shrink and to grow; a clean, formal pairing for describing sectors changing size.
  • defensive spending — money spent repairing or preventing damage, which restores rather than adds.
  • transfer — a shift of money from one party to another in which nothing new is produced.
  • the disagreement dissolves — the dispute stops being a real dispute once the terms are clarified; an elegant way to open a resolution paragraph.
  • to leave the world holding capabilities it lacked — to create something genuinely new; the test for real innovation.
  • to swell the national accounts — to make official economic figures rise; the phrase that separates measured activity from real gain.
  • activity is not prosperity — the essay's named principle: turnover and employment can rise without anyone becoming better off.
  • a record of US$X per kilogram — the highest price ever reached; useful, precise phrasing for commodity data.
  • the actuaries left before the fire, not after it — a model of analytical register: a single concrete detail carrying the whole argument.

6 Self-Review Checklist

  • My introduction paraphrases the prompt, signals both views, AND states my own position (not saved for the conclusion).
  • I gave View A (climate change damages business) a full, sympathetic paragraph with a named principle (risk becoming unpriceable) and one specific example.
  • I gave View B (climate change creates opportunity) a full, sympathetic paragraph with a named principle (a constraint is also an instruction) and one specific example.
  • My position paragraph offers a reasoned resolution (value created versus defended versus transferred) with an example.
  • I kept the essay about BUSINESS — every environmental fact was converted into a cost, price, supply chain or market in the same sentence.
  • I avoided both prophecies — I did not claim climate change will destroy the world economy, nor that green technology will make everyone rich.
  • I did not claim any single disaster was caused by climate change; I wrote about frequency and predictability instead.
  • My conclusion is short, uses a linking word, and introduces no new examples.
