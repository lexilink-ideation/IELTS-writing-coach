C2 Task 2

Unit 1: Sophisticated Argumentative Essay

Skill focus: Task 2 — Hedging and Nuance

1 Mini Lesson

At C2 level, the strongest essays avoid absolute claims. Instead, they use hedging language to present a claim as one reasonable view among several, then reach a balanced, carefully qualified conclusion.

It could be argued that ___. Nevertheless, ___. On balance, it seems reasonable to suggest that ___.

Hedging is not weakness — it signals that you understand the complexity of the issue and can weigh competing considerations.

2 Model Sentences

This example uses a different topic (cities and the countryside) so you can see the pattern — then apply it to the new topic below.

  • It could be argued that countryside life offers a better quality of life than city life, given its lower stress and closer community ties. Nevertheless, this claim arguably understates the economic and educational opportunities that cities provide, particularly for young professionals. On balance, it seems reasonable to suggest that the ideal choice depends heavily on an individual's career stage and personal priorities, rather than any inherent superiority of one setting over the other.

3 Guided Practice

Fill in the gaps using the topic below (not the model paragraph above):

It could be argued that AI tools ___ (claim about homework). Nevertheless, ___ (qualification or counter-consideration). On balance, it seems reasonable to suggest that ___ (nuanced conclusion).

5 Self-Review Checklist

  • I opened with a hedging phrase (e.g. "it could be argued that").
  • I used a concession/contrast connector such as "nevertheless" before my qualification.
  • I closed with a balancing phrase such as "on balance" or "on the whole".
  • My response is about AI and homework.
  • I wrote at least 80 words.
