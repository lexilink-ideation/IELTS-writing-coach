B1 Task 1

Unit 9: Comparing Four Categories

Skill focus: Task 1 — Multi-Category Comparison

1 Mini Lesson

When a table shows four categories, rank them from highest to lowest and use a contrast word to link at least two of them together — this shows the examiner you can handle more complex data than a simple two-category comparison.

The highest figure is ___ (category), at ___%, followed by ___ (category) at ___%. In contrast, ___ (category) is the lowest, at only ___%, whereas ___ (category) sits in the middle at ___%.

Ranking language ("the highest", "followed by", "the lowest") plus a contrast word ("whereas") together let you cover all four categories in one flowing description.

2 Model Sentences

This example uses a different topic (modes of transport) so you can see the pattern — then apply it to the new data below.

  • The highest figure is bus travel, at 45%, followed by car travel at 30%. In contrast, bicycle travel is the lowest, at only 10%, whereas train travel sits in the middle at 15%.

5 Self-Review Checklist

  • My first sentence starts with a capital letter.
  • Every sentence ends with a full stop.
  • I named the highest category using "the highest" or "the most popular".
  • I named the lowest category using "the lowest".
  • I used a contrast connector such as "whereas", "compared to", or "in contrast".
  • I mentioned "books" or "videos".
  • I wrote at least 40 words.
